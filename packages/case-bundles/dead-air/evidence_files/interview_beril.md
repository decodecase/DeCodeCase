# Witness Interview: Beril Yaman

**Interviewee:** Beril Yaman (Sound Engineer, Radyo HERA)
**Interviewer:** Officer Çelik
**Date:** 13.03.2025
**Location:** Radyo HERA, Control Room

---
SPEAKER: Officer Çelik
Ms. Yaman, you were on duty during Mr. Aydın's final broadcast, correct?
---
SPEAKER: Beril Yaman
Yes, I was in the main control room. Onur preferred to run his own board for "Gecenin Sesi" broadcasts, but I was monitoring the levels and the outgoing signal as always.
---
SPEAKER: Officer Çelik
Describe what happened from your perspective.
---
SPEAKER: Beril Yaman
Everything was normal for the first… twelve minutes? He started his monologue, the usual atmospheric music. Then, around 19:57, his voice just… cut out. Mid-sentence. The levels on my board dropped to zero. Not a fade, not a technical glitch I recognized – just silence. The 'On Air' light for Studio 1 went dark.
---
SPEAKER: Officer Çelik
What did you do?
---
SPEAKER: Beril Yaman
I tried to reach him on the intercom. No response. I called his direct line in the studio. Nothing. I radioed Murat, the station manager, who was in his office. He said he'd go check. It took a few minutes… the studio door was locked from the inside. Murat had to use the emergency override.
---
SPEAKER: Officer Çelik
Did you notice anything unusual leading up to the incident? Any strange sounds, any odd behavior from Mr. Aydın?
---
SPEAKER: Beril Yaman
Onur was… more intense than usual that evening. He came in early, spent a lot of time in the studio alone. He asked me to double-check the studio's direct power line and the surge protector, which he'd never done before. He seemed preoccupied. During the sound check, he kept his voice very low, almost whispering. I had to boost his mic gain significantly.
---
SPEAKER: Officer Çelik
You mentioned the 'On Air' light went dark. Does that have a specific technical implication?
---
SPEAKER: Beril Yaman
It means the main broadcast feed from that studio was severed. It could be a catastrophic equipment failure, like the main console dying, or… someone manually cutting the connection from within the studio. Or a power cut specific to the studio. Our backup generator kicked in for the station, but Studio 1 remained dark.
---
SPEAKER: Officer Çelik
From your position, could you see into Studio 1?
---
SPEAKER: Beril Yaman
No, not directly. There's a small window, but the blinds were down. Onur always closed them during his broadcasts. Said it helped him focus.
---
SPEAKER: Officer Çelik
In your experience, what could cause such a sudden and complete audio cutout and power loss to the studio console?
---
SPEAKER: Beril Yaman
A massive electrical surge could do it. If the studio's internal breakers tripped… or if the console itself shorted out catastrophically. The system is designed with safeguards, but… if something bypassed those, or if the fault was severe enough… It's also possible that the main power feed to the console inside the studio was physically disconnected.
---
SPEAKER: Officer Çelik
Did Mr. Aydın ever express concerns about the equipment or safety in the studio?
---
SPEAKER: Beril Yaman
He'd occasionally complain about minor things – a buzzing sound, a sticky fader. Standard stuff. But the request about the surge protector and power line check that day was new. He said something about wanting to ensure a "clean feed" for a particularly sensitive segment. I didn't think much of it at the time.
---
SPEAKER: Officer Çelik
Anything else, Ms. Yaman?
---
SPEAKER: Beril Yaman
When Murat finally got the door open… I heard him shout. I didn't go in. I… I couldn't. The silence from the studio before that, though. That was the worst. Just… dead air.
